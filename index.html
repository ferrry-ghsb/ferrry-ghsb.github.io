About

I am a Human–Computer Interaction researcher specializing in Virtual Reality, multimodal data fusion, and explainable AI — building human-centered intelligent systems that understand and support people in real, situated contexts. My dissertation, An Information Fusion Framework of VR-Based Digital Biomarkers for Early Screening of Mild Cognitive Impairment, develops this approach in clinical decision support, through collaboration with clinical experts (Asan Medical Center, Seoul), engineers at a national research institute (KITECH), and international academic partners (Zurich University of Applied Sciences). I am now extending this foundation to broader situated, embodied, and everyday human-AI interaction contexts.

Research Highlights

VR-Based Digital Biomarkers for Early MCI Screening

A machine learning pipeline classifying Mild Cognitive Impairment from VR-based Instrumental Activities of Daily Living (IADL) behavior — showing that how a person performs an activity, not just whether they complete it, carries clinically meaningful signal.

Virtual kiosk test in action (1:47–2:02)

Explainable, Multimodal Clinical Decision Support

A multimodal reasoning system integrating VR, MRI, and neuropsychological data, including a multi-agent LLM pipeline that keeps AI-generated interpretations transparent and trustworthy to physicians. Supported by an NRF research grant.
